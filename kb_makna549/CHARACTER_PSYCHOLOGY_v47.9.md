I. CORE PRINCIPLE: CONFLICT MADE VISIBLE
Mode A's purpose is to take an internal, psychological conflict (a "Pergolakan Batin") and make it dramatic, realistic, and physically visible. The core conflict is the Protagonist vs. their Internal Enemy—a clear, personified manifestation of their core misbelief (fear, doubt, anxiety). This engine provides the characters and conflict to fill the 5-Beat structure defined in NARRATIVE_STRUCTURE_v47.9.3.md.

II. THE CHARACTER CONFLICT ENGINE: WANT vs. NEED vs. FEAR (EXPANDED)
A compelling character is defined by their internal conflict. Based on advanced narrative theory, this engine creates a triangulation of conflict:

1. The Want (External & Unique):
   - Definition: The conscious, tangible goal the character is chasing throughout the story. This drives the Plot.
   - Function: It must be unique to the character (e.g., "Win the boxing match," "Destroy the Ring," "Get the promotion").
   - The Trap: Achieving the Want often creates a false sense of victory if the Need is ignored.

2. The Need (Internal & Universal):
   - Definition: The psychological or moral truth the character must learn to become whole. This drives the Theme.
   - Function: It is universal and relatable (e.g., "Self-worth comes from within," "Reliance on friends," "Letting go of ego").
   - The Arc: The character typically starts by rejecting the Need in favor of the Want.

3. The Fear (The Internal Obstacle):
   - Definition: The deep-seated Lie or Misbelief that prevents the character from accepting their Need.
   - Function: It creates the friction. The "Internal Enemy" is the personification of this Fear.

II.B. THE WANT vs. NEED DIAGNOSTIC MATRIX (THE 5 DIFFERENCES)
// Use this matrix to VALIDATE the Want/Need defined above. Ensure they are distinct.

1. Internal vs. External:
   - Want: External (Plot). Frodo wants to destroy the Ring.
   - Need: Internal (Theme). Frodo needs to realize he cannot do it alone.

2. The Obstacle Source:
   - Want Obstacle: External (Villains, locked doors).
   - Need Obstacle: Internal (Ego, Fear, "The Sheriff who doesn't share").

3. Awareness:
   - Want: Known. The character shouts it.
   - Need: Unknown/Rejected. The character actively fights it.

4. The Function:
   - Want: The Fun (Explosions, heists).
   - Need: The Heart (Emotional connection).

5. Universality:
   - Want: Unique to Character.
   - Need: Universal to Audience.

III. THE CTA-CENTRIC ARC (MANDATORY)
For this system, the character's journey must be engineered to align with the Call to Action (CTA).

1. Define the CTA: What is the single action you want the audience to take?
2. Engineer the Pain Point: The character's core Fear/Pain Point must be a problem that the CTA directly solves.
3. Engineer the Aspiration: The character's core Want/Aspiration must be the state they will achieve after completing the CTA.

IV. THE 3D CHARACTER FRAMEWORK
To make a character feel real, they must be built across three dimensions.

1. Physiology (The External): Appearance, Style, Mannerisms.
2. Psychology (The Internal): Temperament, Archetype, Internal Conflict.
3. Sociology (The Contextual): Background, Relationships, Beliefs.

VI.A. SYMBOLIC VISUALIZATION PROTOCOL (NEW - EXPANDED)

1. Object Symbolism (The Totem):
   - Assign a specific physical object to represent the character's core conflict or emotional state.
   - Mechanism: The object evolves with the character.
   - Examples:
     * Guilt: A stain on a shirt that grows larger or changes color, no matter how much they scrub (Lady Macbeth style).
     * Insecurity: A mask the character wears that starts to crack or fuse to their face.
     * Freedom: A bird in a cage seen in the background; at the climax, the cage is open.
   - Application: Identify one "Totem Object" in Stage 7 (Character Definition) that anchors the conflict.

2. Color Symbolism (The Mood Ring):
   - Use color shifts to signal internal state changes without dialogue.
   - Mechanism: Shift the lighting or grading based on the dominant emotion.
   - Examples:
     * Obsession/Envy: Sickly Green (Vertigo style).
     * Danger/Anger: Harsh Red.
     * Isolation/Melancholy: Cold Blue/Grey.
     * Hope/Clarity: Warm Gold/Amber (breaking through the cold).
   - Application: Bake these color cues directly into the PROMPT_SYSTEM scene_elements.atmosphere.

3. Action Symbolism (The Ritual):
   - A repetitive physical action that reveals the character's psyche.
   - Mechanism: An unconscious habit that intensifies with stress.
   - Examples:
     * Anxiety: Compulsively arranging pens in perfect alignment.
     * Repression: Constantly buttoning up a coat or checking a lock.
     * Liberation: Loosening a tie, taking off shoes, messing up hair.
   - Application: Script these "Micro-Actions" in the Visual Action Prompt to show emotion physically.

VI.B. THE 4-STEP SYMBOL CONSTRUCTION PROTOCOL
// Transcribed from StudioBinder.
// How to build a symbol from scratch.

Step 1: Identify the Theme:
   - What is the intangible idea? (e.g., Nostalgia, Guilt, Freedom).
   - Selection: Choose a physical object to represent it (e.g., "Wild Strawberries" = Nostalgia).

Step 2: Decide Obviousness:
   - Integral Symbol: Must be understood to follow the plot (e.g., The Ring in LOTR). Make it loud.
   - Subtextual Symbol: Adds depth but not required for plot (e.g., The color Red in Sixth Sense). Keep it subtle.

Step 3: The Introduction:
   - How we first see the symbol defines its meaning.
   - Example: Ghost Dog is introduced reading "Way of the Samurai" alone. Symbol established: Dignity/Code.

Step 4: The Recurrence (The Rule of 3):
   - The symbol must appear at least 3 times, changing slightly each time to reflect the character's inner state.
   - Occurrence 1: Establishment.
   - Occurrence 2: Complication (The symbol is threatened/damaged).
   - Occurrence 3: Resolution (The symbol is discarded, fixed, or given away).

VII. ADVANCED EMOTIONAL MECHANICS (NEW - EXPANDED)
// Based on Emotion Theory (StudioBinder & Think Story).
// This section guides HOW the audience feels what the character feels.

1. Cognitive Appraisal Theory (The Perception Filter)
   - Emotions in the script must not come from the event itself, but from the character's appraisal (interpretation) of the event.
   - Mechanism: Event -> [Character's Insecurity/Fear] -> Distorted Perception -> Emotion.
   - Example: A colleague says "Nice work."
     * Normal Appraisal: Feels Happy/Proud.
     * Imposter Syndrome Appraisal: "They are being sarcastic because they know I'm a fraud." -> Feels Shame/Panic.
   - Application: Ensure the "Internal Enemy" actively corrupts the character's appraisal of neutral events in the script.

2. Recontextualized Repetition (The Emotional Echo)
   - To create deep emotion quickly, use a specific object, phrase, or action twice with different meanings.
   - Setup (The Want): The element appears early, linked to the character's False Belief or external Want. (e.g., A "Success Trophy" that the character polishes obsessively).
   - Payoff (The Need): The same element appears at the climax/resolution, but its meaning has shifted to reflect the internal Need. (e.g., The character leaves the "Success Trophy" gathering dust to go play with their child).
   - Application: Every script must identify one "Echo Element" to anchor the emotional arc.

3. The "Realization" Moment
   - The emotional climax is not the external victory, but the moment of internal realization.
   - The Shift: The moment the character stops fighting the Obstacle (Fear) and accepts the Need.
   - Visualizing the Shift: In Mode A, this is often the moment the "Internal Enemy" is silenced, dissolved, or embraced—not destroyed by force, but rendered powerless by the truth.

4. RECONTEXTUALIZED REPETITION (THE EMOTIONAL ECHO)
   // Transcribed from StudioBinder.
   // A specific technique to create deep emotion without dialogue.
   - The Technique: "Putting a line, object, or person in a new setting that gives it a more profound significance."
   - Execution Steps:
     * Step 1 (The Setup - Joy/Neutral): Show an object/action in a positive or neutral context early in the script. (e.g., Toy Story 2: The tree swing when Emily is young and happy).
     * Step 2 (The Gap): Time passes. The context changes (Loss, failure, aging).
     * Step 3 (The Payoff - Tragedy/Meaning): Show the exact same object/action again. The audience's memory fills the gap with emotion. (e.g., The empty tree swing / The tire).
   - Mandate: Every Mode A script must contain ONE instance of Recontextualized Repetition.
