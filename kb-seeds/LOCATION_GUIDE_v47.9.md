# LOCATION GUIDE v47.9.6 (Dynamic & Expanded)

// The Location Catalyst Protocol: Defining the "where" of the narrative.
// v47.9.6: Upgraded from v47.9. Includes Hyper-Expansion (Section IV).

### I. CORE PRINCIPLE
In the MAKNA engine, a location is never passive. It is an active catalyst for the story. The "where" is chosen to maximize the narrative's core conflict, whether that conflict is psychological (Psychodrama) or external (Realist-Viral).

### II. STATIC INSPIRATION BANK
// This is the default static list of options. It is an "Inspiration Bank," not a complete menu.
// The system will use the "Dynamic Generation Mandate" (Section III) if the user requests alternatives.

**CATEGORY 1: NORMAL (Contemporary & Relatable)**
*Function: Creates a baseline of reality. The conflict comes from a familiar situation.*
* A sterile, quiet office after hours.
* A crowded, noisy coffee shop.
* A messy, lived-in bedroom or apartment.
* A gridlocked city street during rush hour.
* A fluorescent-lit supermarket aisle.
* A quiet suburban park at dusk.
* A high-school classroom or university lecture hall.

**CATEGORY 2: FANTASY & SCI-FI (Imaginative & World-Building)**
*Function: Establishes a world with different rules, allowing for high-concept conflicts.*
* The glowing, bioluminescent caves of a lost world.
* A neon-drenched, rainy cyberpunk alley.
* The derelict, floating bridge of a ghost spaceship.
* A crumbling, ancient mountaintop temple.
* A mystical forest with shimmering, impossible flora.
* The colossal, ancient library of a forgotten civilization.
* A city built on the back of a giant creature.

**CATEGORY 3: EXTREME & PSYCHODRAMA (Symbolic & Unsettling)**
*Function: Visualizes the character's internal state. The location is the emotion.*
* An endless, sterile hospital corridor.
* A soundproof interrogation room with a two-way mirror.
* A room where the walls are physically closing in.
* A checkered ballroom that stretches into infinity.
* A disorienting forest shrouded in thick, unnatural fog.
* A soundstage under a single, harsh interrogation light.
* A frozen, desolate tundra at twilight.
* The edge of a volcanic caldera.

**CATEGORY 4: HISTORICAL EVENTS (High-Stakes & Thematic)**
*Function: Uses a high-stakes historical or future context to frame the narrative.*
* **Ancient & Mythological:**
    * A Gladiator combat arena (e.g., The Colosseum).
    * The battlefield of a Crusade (e.g., Siege of Jerusalem).
    * The Battle of Babad (Nusantara conflict).
    * Ancient Greece during a philosophical debate.
    * The construction site of the Great Pyramids.
    * A marketplace during Prophetic eras (e.g., Badar).
    * The Cretaceous period, moments before extinction.
* **War & Conflict:**
    * A trench during World War 1.
    * A command bunker during World War 2.
    * The colonial era (e.g., Dutch/Japanese occupation).
* **Future & Dystopian:**
    * A desolate, post-nuclear war wasteland.
    * The first human colony on Mars.
    * A futuristic city during a high-tech civil war.

### III. DYNAMIC GENERATION MANDATE (PROTOCOL)
// This protocol activates when the user requests "alternative options" or "something else."
// This protocol is non-negotiable and forbids the system from simply repeating the static list from Section II.

1.  **Protocol Activation:** User requests alternative options.
2.  **Data Retrieval (Mandatory):** The system must retrieve the core strategic data from the workflow:
    * Core Conflict (e.g., "Imposter Syndrome - Pinjam Duit Context")
    * Audience Profile (e.g., "Gen Z Indonesia")
    * Hot Trend (e.g., "Flexing Culture")
3.  **Dynamic Synthesis & Generation:** The system must synthesize this data to invent new, relevant Location options on the fly.
4.  **Presentation:** The system will present these new, dynamically generated options to the user.

**EXECUTION EXAMPLE:**
* **Data:** Audience = Gen Z Indonesia, Conflict = "Imposter Syndrome (Pinjam Duit Context)".
* **User:** "I don't like these locations. Give me something different."
* **System (Activating Dynamic Protocol):**
    "Understood. Based on your specific conflict (Gen Z, Flexing, Pinjam Duit), here are three dynamically generated locations:
    1.  **'Warkop' (Warung Kopi) Patio:** A realistic social setting. The Protagonist ('flexing' with a laptop) sits across from an 'Antagonist' (old friend) who just orders plain tea, creating intense, unspoken social pressure.
    2.  **'ATM Queue (Payday)':** A high-anxiety, realistic location. The Protagonist feels the 'Skeptic' (person behind them) is judging the amount of cash they are withdrawing.
    3.  **'Esthetic Coffeeshop':** A visually beautiful location, but the 'Enemy' is the sound design. Every item on the menu is a 'ping' from a 'pinjam duit' text message, creating a dramatic contrast."
