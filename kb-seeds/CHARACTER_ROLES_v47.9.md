I. CORE PRINCIPLE: EXTERNAL CONFLICT & TOTAL REALISM
This framework is Mode B. Its purpose is to build a narrative based on 100% realistic, external conflict.
The story is driven by a Protagonist (the hero) with a clear goal, who is opposed by a concrete, physical Antagonist (the villain). All abstract concepts are forbidden.

II. THE PRIMARY ROLES (Mandatory)

1. The Protagonist (The Hero)
   - Function: The audience surrogate. The character the viewer roots for.
   - Mandate: Must have a clear, tangible, and visible goal. Their pain points and desires must be relatable.
   - CTA Link: The Protagonist's transformation (from pain to success) is the primary driver for the Call to Action (CTA).

2. The Antagonist (The Villain / The Obstacle)
   - Function: The character or force that directly opposes the Protagonist and creates the conflict.
   - Mandate: The Antagonist must be a concrete, physical, and realistic entity. Their goal must be in direct conflict with the Protagonist's goal.
   - Types:
     * Personified: A physical person, a rival, a bully, a corrupt boss.
     * Abstract (See Sec. IV): A system, a corporation, a societal pressure (which must be personified).

III. THE SUPPORTING ROLES (Optional)
These roles add depth and complexity to the central conflict.

1. The Sidekick (The Ally)
   - Function: Supports the Protagonist. Provides encouragement, comic relief, or a second set of hands.
   - Example: A loyal friend, a helpful colleague, a pet.

2. The Foil
   - Function: A character who contrasts with the Protagonist to highlight their specific traits. They are not necessarily an enemy, but their presence complicates the story.
   - Example: A cautious, by-the-rules colleague who questions the Protagonist's risky (but correct) plan.

3. The Guardian (The Mentor)
   - Function: Protects the Protagonist or provides them with the tool/knowledge to win.
   - Note: In advertising, the Product or Brand is often cast in this role.

4. The Contagonist (The Tempter)
   - Function: An ally who seems helpful but ultimately supports the conflict, often tempting the Protagonist to take the easy way out or give up.
   - Example: A friend who says, "Maybe this is too hard. You should just quit and come have a drink."

5. The Skeptic
   - Function: A character who openly doubts the Protagonist or the goal. They are hostile to the hero's journey.
   - Example: A family member who says, "Your new business idea is stupid and will never work."

V. THE 3 LAYERS OF CONFLICT MATRIX (NEW)
// Based on StudioBinder Conflict Theory.
// Use this matrix to ensure the Antagonist/Conflict operates on multiple levels for maximum engagement.

To build a rich narrative, the conflict should ideally hit at least two of these three layers:

1. Inner Conflict (The Psychological Layer)
   - Definition: The battle within the character (Identity, Fear, Moral Dilemma).
   - Connection: This links Mode B to Mode A. Even in an external story, the hero hesitates.
   - Example: The Hero wants to confront the Boss (External), but fights their own "People Pleaser" nature (Inner).

2. Personal Conflict (The Interpersonal Layer)
   - Definition: Direct friction between specific characters (Protagonist vs. Antagonist/Rival/Family).
   - Focus: Relationships, Ego, specific Opponents.
   - Example: The Hero vs. The Rival Colleague who wants the same promotion.

3. Extrapersonal Conflict (The Environmental Layer)
   - Definition: Obstacles from outside human control or massive systems (Nature, Society, Technology, Time).
   - Focus: Survival, Endurance, "The System."
   - Example: The Hero vs. A crashing stock market, a deadline (Time), or a traffic jam (Environment).

Strategic Application:
   - The "Double-Punch" Strategy: Combine layers for impact. (e.g., Hero fights a Rival (Personal) during a massive Deadline/Crisis (Extrapersonal)).
   - The "Mirror" Strategy: The Personal Conflict reflects the Inner Conflict. (e.g., The Hero hates their Boss (Personal) because the Boss represents the Hero's own fear of failure (Inner)).

**SET A: PASSIVE-AGGRESSIVE (The "Polite" War)**

4.  **"The Weaponized Incompetence":** "Aku gak bisa, kamu aja yang bikin (padahal malas)."
5.  **"The Guilt Trip":** "Aku udah korbankan semuanya buat kamu, tapi ini balasanmu?"

7.  **"The Concern Troll":** "Aku ngomong gini demi kebaikan kamu lho." (I'm saying this for your own good).
8.  **"The Moving Goalpost":** Whatever the Hero achieves, the standard is raised higher.
9.  **"The Triangulation":** Bringing a 3rd person into the argument to gang up on the Hero.
10. **"The Victim Card":** The Attacker cries/plays victim as soon as the Hero defends themselves.
