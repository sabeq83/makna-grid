I. CORE PRINCIPLE
An "Auteur" is the guiding creative voice of the project. Selecting an Auteur is a technical choice that defines the project's overall mood, pacing, cinematic language, and narrative approach. This guide provides clear, concrete examples to lock in a specific, reproducible style. This selection directly informs the artistic_vision, composition, lighting, and audio parameters in the PROMPT_SYSTEM_v47.9.3.md.

I.B. THE 3 PREMISES OF AUTEUR THEORY (Andrew Sarris)
// Use this to justify WHY an Auteur is selected.

1. Technical Competence: The director must know the rules (lighting, blocking) before breaking them. (We assume AI has this).
2. Distinguishable Personality: A recurrent style (visuals, stories, casts) that serves as a signature. "The way a film looks and moves should have a relationship to the way a director thinks and feels."
3. Interior Meaning: The ultimate premise. The tension between the director's personality and their material.

CATEGORY 1: FILM DIRECTORS (Narrative & Cinematic Focus)

1. David Fincher
   - Style: Dark, meticulous, and moody psychological thrillers.
   - Visuals: Desaturated, cold digital palettes (sickly greens, cold blues), high-contrast shadows, precise and unnerving camera movements.
   - Application: Perfect for Psychodrama, Imposter Syndrome, and any narrative requiring a tense, oppressive, and claustrophobic atmosphere.
   - Concrete Example: The cold, calculating, and desaturated look of *The Social Network* or the oppressive darkness of *Se7en*.

2. Wes Anderson
   - Style: Quirky, symmetrical, and emotionally deadpan.
   - Visuals: Perfect center-framed symmetry, distinct pastel color palettes (pinks, yellows, baby blues), flat compositions, and whimsical details.
   - Application: Ideal for creating an unsettling contrast (Mode B) or a quirky, absurd narrative. Used to show a perfect world being disrupted.
   - Concrete Example: The perfect symmetry and pastel color story of *The Grand Budapest Hotel*.

3. Christopher Nolan
   - Style: Epic-scale, practical realism, and complex narratives.
   - Visuals: Grand, wide-angle IMAX-style shots, cool or naturalistic color palettes, focus on architectural scale and practical effects.
   - Application: Staging the character's mind as an epic, architectural battlefield (like in Inception) or treating an abstract threat with terrifying, large-scale realism.
   - Concrete Example: The dream-bending practical effects of *Inception* or the terrifying, grand-scale realism of *Oppenheimer*.

4. Denis Villeneuve
   - Style: Atmospheric, brutalist, and masterful suspense.
   - Visuals: Powerful, silhouette-heavy compositions (often via Roger Deakins), dramatic and often monochromatic palettes (e.g., suffocating orange fog, grey/blue), and a sense of immense, alien scale.
   - Application: Building unbearable dread and atmosphere. Perfect for sci-fi, horror, or any story where the environment itself is an antagonist.
   - Concrete Example: The alien, atmospheric, and silhouette-heavy compositions of *Arrival* or *Blade Runner 2049*.

5. Quentin Tarantino
   - Style: Stylized, dialogue-heavy, and non-linear.
   - Visuals: Sharp, high-contrast, and dynamic camera work (including iconic trunk shots and long takes), pop-culture references, and sudden bursts of stylized violence.
   - Application: Staging psychological confrontations as tense, dialogue-driven "standoffs" that erupt into cathartic (metaphorical) violence.
   - Concrete Example: The stylish, dialogue-driven suspense scenes in *Pulp Fiction* or *Inglourious Basterds*.

6. Wong Kar-wai (The Master of Longing)
   - Themes: Memory, passage of time, lost love, solitude in a crowd.
   - Visual Signature (The Christopher Doyle Look):
     * Step-Printing: Ghostly slow-motion (low frame rate printed multiple times) to visualize "memory blur."
     * Foreground Obstruction: Frames within frames (shooting through doorways, windows, hallways) to show isolation/voyeurism.
     * Saturation: Deep Reds/Ambers (Desire) or Queasy Teals/Greens (Alienation).
     * Hands: "Hands betray what faces hide." Focus on tactile details (smoke, glass, hem of a dress).
   - Style: Romantic melancholy, loneliness, and dreamlike motion.
   - Visuals: Saturated, neon-drenched cityscapes, smoky rooms, step-printing (ghostly slow-motion), and intimate, rain-streaked close-ups.
   - Application: Visualizing internal conflicts of loneliness, lost love, or nostalgia. The "Internal Enemy" is a beautiful, sorrowful ghost.
   - Concrete Example: The neon-soaked, dreamlike melancholy of *Chungking Express* or *In the Mood for Love*.

7. Guy Ritchie
   - Style: Kinetic, stylish, and fast-paced editing.
   - Visuals: Quick-cut montages, speed ramps, dynamic camera movements, and layered, energetic sound design.
   - Application: Perfect for visualizing a character's "overthinking" or "Notification Anxiety" as a chaotic, fast-paced montage of overwhelming information.
   - Concrete Example: The rapid-fire "pre-fight visualization" scenes in *Sherlock Holmes* or the stylish heist montages in *Snatch*.

8. Steven Spielberg
   - Style: Blockbuster spectacle, wonder, and humanistic emotion.
   - Visuals: Grand, sweeping camera moves, visible shafts of light ("God rays"), and the iconic "Spielberg Face" (awe-filled close-ups).
   - Application: Portraying the "Internal Enemy" as a massive, blockbuster monster (like the T-Rex) or showing the solution with a sense of wonder and awe.
   - Concrete Example: The sense of awe and terror in *Jurassic Park* or the wonder in *E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial*.

9. Yorgos Lanthimos (NEW)
   - Style: Surreal, absurdist, and psychologically uncomfortable.
   - Visuals: Wide-angle/Fish-eye lenses, clinical lighting, awkward framing, and deadpan performances.
   - Application: Perfect for visualizing social absurdity or "The System" as a bizarre, arbitrary set of rules. Great for "weird" ad concepts.
   - Concrete Example: The uncanny, clinical surrealism of *The Lobster* or *Poor Things*.

10. Sofia Coppola (NEW)
    - Style: Ethereal, atmospheric, and deeply introspective.
    - Visuals: Soft focus, pastel/muted tones, natural light, and a "dreamy" gaze emphasizing isolation.
    - Application: Ideal for Mode A (Psychodrama) focusing on female protagonists, loneliness, or "Gilded Cage" scenarios (e.g., trapped in a luxury lifestyle).
    - Concrete Example: The dreamy isolation of *Lost in Translation* or *Marie Antoinette*.

11. Jean-Luc Godard / French New Wave (NEW)
    - Style: Rebellious, rule-breaking, and avant-garde.
    - Visuals: Jump cuts, handheld camera, breaking the fourth wall, and guerrilla filmmaking aesthetics.
    - Application: For "Mode B" narratives that challenge the status quo or disrupt conventional storytelling.
    - Concrete Example: The frantic, rule-breaking energy of *Breathless*.

12. Hayao Miyazaki (NEW)
    - Style: Whimsical, environmental, and deeply humanistic.
    - Visuals: Lush, vibrant hand-drawn aesthetics (or AI equivalent), obsession with flight/wind, and detailed natural environments.
    - Application: Visualizing "Escape" or "Wonder." Great for softening a heavy topic with magical realism.
    - Concrete Example: The immersive, magical world of *Spirited Away* or *The Wind Rises*.

13. Ridley Scott (The World Builder)
    - Core Philosophy: "The environment is a character."
    - Visual Signature:
      * The "Shimmer" Light: Moving lights reflected off water/mirrors onto walls. Adds a "subconscious" layer of movement and unease.
      * Retina Response (Eye Light): A specific, small light source (a "pup") reflected in the actor's eyes. In *Blade Runner*, this makes Replicants look "alive but alien."
      * Scale: Massive architecture vs. small humans.
      * Atmosphere: Rain, smoke, steam, neon. "Texture in the air."

CATEGORY 2: CONTENT CREATORS (Pacing & Retention Focus)

1. MrBeast (YouTube Channel)
   - Style: Extreme retention-focused, high-energy challenges.
   - Visuals: Bright, saturated colors, rapid-fire cuts, on-screen text/graphics, and a relentless focus on the "challenge" or "stakes."
   - Application: Structuring the narrative as a high-stakes, fast-paced challenge with a clear, massive payoff. All visual choices serve retention.
   - Concrete Example: The pacing and visual style of any MrBeast Challenge Video (e.g., "Surviving 50 Hours in...").

2. Joe Rogan (Podcast Channel)
   - Style: Long-form, intimate, and conversational.
   - Visuals: High-quality "podcast" aesthetic (see VISUAL_STYLE_GUIDE_v47.9.md), cinematic shallow depth-of-field, high-quality microphones, multi-camera setup.
   - Application: Staging the narrative as a deep, intimate conversation, either with the audience, another character, or even the "Enemy."
   - Concrete Example: The visual setup of *The Joe Rogan Experience* podcast.

3. Johnny Harris (YouTube Channel)
   - Style: High-production video essays and investigative journalism.
   - Visuals: Cinematic-quality footage, integrated motion graphics, "run and gun" handheld feel, and a personal, investigative tone.
   - Application: Framing the narrative as a "video investigation" into the character's own problem, uncovering the "truth" (the solution).
   - Concrete Example: The visual storytelling and motion graphics in any Johnny Harris or Vox Borders video essay.

### IV. THE AUTEUR SIGNATURE MOVE MATRIX (30 DIRECTORIAL TECHNIQUES)
// MANDATE: Don't just copy the "Look". Execute the specific "Move" that defines the director's brain.

**SET A: CAMERA BLOCKING & MOVEMENT (The Physical Signature)**
1.  **"The Spielberg Oner":** A single long take that moves from Wide to Close-Up without cutting, driven by character movement.
2.  **"The Bayhem 360":** Low angle, spinning circular tracking shot around a standing hero (Heroic/Dizzying).
3.  **"The Fincher Tilt":** Camera matches the character's movement speed perfectly. If they stop, camera stops instantly. Clinical precision.
4.  **"The Tarantino Trunk Shot":** Extremely low angle looking up from inside a trunk/box at characters looking down.
5.  **"The Hitchcock Zoom (Vertigo)":** Dolly In + Zoom Out. Background warps, subject stays same size. (Realization of horror).
6.  **"The Sorkin Walk-and-Talk":** Tracking shot moving backward fast through corridors while characters argue rapidly.
7.  **"The Wes Anderson Planimetric":** Camera moves strictly at 90-degree angles (Pan/Tilt/Truck) like a 2D platformer game.
8.  **"The Raimi Cam (Evil Dead)":** Camera becomes the "Entity", rushing violently towards the screaming subject (FPV style).
9.  **"The Nolan Rotation":** Camera rotates heavily on the Z-axis (Dutch Roll) to simulate a world turning upside down.
10. **"The Kubrick Stare":** Camera pushes in slowly on a character with head tilted down, eyes looking up.

**SET B: EDITING & TEMPORAL LOGIC (The Time Signature)**
11. **"The Wright Smash (Edgar Wright)":** Mundane actions (pouring coffee) edited as violent, fast-cut action sequences with loud SFX.
12. **"The Wong Kar-Wai Step":** Low shutter speed (6fps) printed to 24fps. Motion creates a ghostly, streaky blur while time feels slow.
13. **"The Inarritu Stitch":** Invisible cuts hidden in whip-pans or object passing, creating the illusion of one continuous infinite take.
14. **"The Guy Ritchie Explain":** Fast-forward montage with voiceover explaining the plan, then rewinding to "Now".
15. **"The Lynchian Pause":** Holding a shot 5 seconds too long until it becomes uncomfortable and uncanny.
16. **"The French Jump Cut (Godard)":** Cutting time out of a single shot, making the subject "jump" forward. Jarring energy.
17. **"The Parallel Crescendo (Nolan)":** Cross-cutting between 3 different timelines that all climax at the exact same second.
18. **"The Match-Dissolve":** Fading one face over another to imply connection or time passing (Titanic style).
19. **"The Freeze-Frame Narration (Scorsese)":** Action stops dead. Voiceover: "Yeah, that's me."
20. **"The Subliminal Flash":** Single-frame insert of a demonic/scary image (Exorcist style) to trigger subconscious fear.

**SET C: FRAMING & BLOCKING (The Visual Signature)**
21. **"The Kurosawa Weather":** Using rain/wind/fog to physically obstruct the actors and heighten drama.
22. **"The Deakins Silhouette":** Lighting the background, leaving the subject in total darkness.
23. **"The Ozu Tatami Shot":** Camera placed specifically 3 feet off the ground (kneeling height). Quiet observation.
24. **"The Refn Neon":** Frame dominated by a single monochromatic neon color (Red/Blue).
25. **"The Ford Doorway":** Framing the hero through a dark doorway or window frame (Isolation).
26. **"The Split-Diopter (De Palma)":** Foregound object and Background object are both razor sharp (using special lens). unnatural focus.
27. **"The Center-Point Perspective":** All perspective lines converge exactly on the subject's forehead.
28. **"The Negative Space (Mr. Robot)":** Subject pushed to the extreme bottom-corner of the frame. "Headroom" is massive and oppressive.
29. **"The Mirror Fracture":** Shooting the subject only through reflections in broken or multiple mirrors.
30. **"The Fourth Wall Break (Fleabag)":** Character looks directly at the lens for a split second to share a secret with the audience.
